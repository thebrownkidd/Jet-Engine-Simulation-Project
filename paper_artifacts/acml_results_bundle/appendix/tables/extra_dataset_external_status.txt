                                         feature table (per-channel time/freq
                                         features) at
                                         data/processed/ims_bearing_features.csv
  2. experiments/acml/exp_ims_bearing.py bounded k-latent AE vs unbounded AE vs

Headline outcome (16 bearing-channel units, 10 train / 6 test)
  - STABILITY CLAIM REPRODUCES: sensor-space VAR is unstable (rho=1.016>1) and
    diverges in free run (growth ~181x, unbounded), while the bounded latent
    rollout stays bounded (growth ~1.5-2x) -- the central algorithmic result
    holds on an independent dataset.
  - RECONSTRUCTION / K-AS-REGULARIZER REPRODUCES for k>=3 (recon R2 ~0.82-0.83);
    k=2 is an unstable operating point on this feature space (recon collapses),
    so bearings need slightly higher latent capacity than turbofan.
  - RUL UTILITY REPRODUCES for k>=3 (RMSE 19.4 < mean-baseline 22.8, R2 ~0.28);
    at k=2 it does not beat the baseline.
  - MONOTONICITY DOES NOT TRANSFER: bearing degradation is abrupt (healthy
    plateau then sudden spike), giving ~50% monotone-violation. The monotone-
    health assumption is turbofan-specific; boundedness/stability is not.

Interpretation for the paper: the bounded-latent stability guarantee generalizes
across degradation domains; the monotone-progression modelling choice is
domain-dependent and should be presented as such rather than as universal.

SECOND EXTERNAL DATASET: NASA Li-ion battery aging (data/raw_battery)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Pipeline
  1. experiments/acml/battery_prep.py    per-discharge-cycle features
                                         (capacity fade + V/I/T curve summaries)
                                         -> data/processed/battery_features.csv
  2. experiments/acml/exp_ims_bearing.py --features .../battery_features.csv
                                         --name battery  (generic runner)

Artifacts
  results/acml/tables/battery_summary.csv (+ .tex)
  results/acml/tables/battery_k_sweep.csv (+ .tex)
  results/acml/figures/battery_summary.png

Headline outcome (26 batteries, 17 train / 9 test)
  - STABILITY DIFFERENTIATOR DOES NOT APPLY HERE: the per-discharge-cycle
    feature dynamics are intrinsically slow/stable, so the sensor-space VAR is
    ALREADY bounded (rho=1.003, growth ~1.3x). There is no divergence to
    prevent, so the bounded-vs-diverging contrast that held on turbofan
    (rho>1, 25-760x) and IMS bearings (VAR 181x) collapses. This delimits the
    scope of the stability claim: it matters only when the raw/sensor rollout
    is actually unstable (rho>1).
  - RECONSTRUCTION reproduces only at higher capacity (k>=4, recon R2 ~0.80);
    k<=2 collapses. Battery groups span multiple temperatures (4/24/43 C) and
    load protocols (CC / square-wave / mixed) with no regime normalization, so
    a random unit split induces cross-group domain shift that a 2-D latent
    cannot absorb.
  - RUL is weak: best RMSE ~36 (k=5) barely beats the mean baseline ~45; at
    k=2 it does not beat the baseline. Heterogeneous per-group lifetimes
    (40 to 197 cycles) make pooled RUL hard without group conditioning.
  - MONOTONICITY: ~51% violation at k=2, but this reflects the degenerate k=2
    latent under domain shift (negative recon), not the underlying capacity
    signal, which is monotone per battery.

Cross-dataset conclusion for the paper
  The three domains form a clean scope statement:
    * turbofan (C-MAPSS): sensor VAR unstable (rho>1) -> bounded latent wins.
    * bearings (IMS):      feature VAR unstable (rho>1) -> bounded latent wins,
                           but monotonicity fails (abrupt failure).
    * batteries (NASA):    feature VAR already stable (rho~1) -> no stability
                           gap; heterogeneity/regime shift dominates instead.
  The bounded-latent stability guarantee is real but conditional on rho>1 in the
  competing sensor-space rollout; it is not a universal improvement.

FINAL CROSS-DOMAIN SCOPE (four datasets)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * turbofan (C-MAPSS):  VAR rho>1 -> bounded latent wins; monotone; RUL strong.
  * PHM milling:         VAR rho>1 (797x) -> bounded latent wins; monotone wear;
                         RUL ~2.4x better than baseline. FULL reproduction.
  * IMS bearings:        VAR rho>1 (181x) -> bounded latent wins; recon/RUL need
                         k>=3; monotonicity fails (abrupt failure).
  * NASA battery:        VAR rho~1 (stable) -> no stability gap to exploit;
                         cross-group heterogeneity dominates.
  Takeaway: the bounded-latent stability guarantee reproduces whenever the
  competing sensor-space rollout is unstable (rho>1), and is accompanied by
  monotone health + strong RUL when degradation is gradual (turbofan, milling).
  It offers no advantage when sensor dynamics are already stable (battery), and
  the monotone-health assumption is domain-dependent (fails for abrupt bearing
  failure).

FOURTH EXTERNAL DATASET: Beijing multi-site air quality (forecasting; NO RUL)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Objective differs: this is a stationary multivariate forecasting problem (12
stations, hourly, 11 variables), not run-to-failure. We test ONLY the core
contributions that apply -- reconstruction, stable rollout, forecastability --
and drop RUL and monotone-health (the AE is trained with lambda_mono=0).

Pipeline
  1. experiments/acml/air_quality_prep.py   12 stations -> long table (stride=3h)
                                            data/processed/air_quality_features.csv
  2. experiments/acml/exp_air_quality.py    bounded/unbounded AE, sensor VAR,
                                            forecast skill vs persistence, K-sweep.

Artifacts
  results/acml/tables/air_quality_summary.csv (+ .tex)
  results/acml/tables/air_quality_k_sweep.csv (+ .tex)
  results/acml/figures/air_quality_summary.png

Headline outcome (12 stations, 8 train / 4 test) -- SCOPE-LIMITING (honest)
  - RECONSTRUCTION reproduces: bounded k=2 recon R2 = 0.806, rising to 0.90 at
    k>=4. A compact latent still captures the multivariate state.
  - STABILITY CONTRAST REVERSES: because the series are stationary/mean-
    reverting, the sensor-space VAR is contractive (rho=0.996<1, free-run norm
    decays to ~0.01) -- there is NO divergence to prevent. Meanwhile the
    constant-velocity latent rollout drifts to a latent-box corner and the
    decoded standardized norm grows ~9.5x. The latent itself is bounded by
    construction (||h||<=sqrt(k)); it is the CV extrapolation + decode that
    moves, not a violation of the boundedness theorem.
  - FORECASTABILITY FAILS: constant-velocity latent forecasts are worse than
    persistence at every horizon (skill h1=-0.34, h8=-0.71, h24=-2.69). CV
    extrapolation is the wrong inductive bias for cyclic / mean-reverting data.

Interpretation: the method's rollout model assumes a slowly-varying, trending
(degradation) latent with an UNSTABLE competing sensor rollout. Stationary
forecasting violates both premises. The reusable, transferable part here is the
bounded AUTOENCODER (reconstruction), not the constant-velocity forecast.

FINAL CROSS-DOMAIN SCOPE (five datasets)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  DEGRADATION with UNSTABLE sensor dynamics (rho>1) -- method works:
    * turbofan (C-MAPSS): bounded latent wins; monotone; RUL strong.
    * PHM milling:        VAR 797x -> bounded latent wins; monotone wear;
                          RUL ~2.4x better. FULL reproduction.
    * IMS bearings:       VAR 181x -> bounded latent wins; recon/RUL need k>=3;
                          monotonicity fails (abrupt failure).
  DEGRADATION with STABLE sensor dynamics (rho~1) -- no stability gap:
    * NASA battery:       VAR already stable; heterogeneity dominates.
  STATIONARY FORECASTING (rho<1, cyclic) -- core rollout does not apply:
    * Beijing air quality: VAR contractive; CV rollout drifts and loses to
                           persistence; only reconstruction transfers.

  UNIFIED STATEMENT: the bounded-latent stable-rollout contribution reproduces
  precisely when (i) degradation is gradual/trending and (ii) the competing
  sensor-space rollout is unstable (rho>1). It provides no benefit when sensor
  dynamics are already stable, and the constant-velocity rollout is unsuited to
  stationary/cyclic forecasting. Reconstruction (the bounded AE) transfers
  universally; the stability advantage and monotone-health prior are
  regime-specific.
