Subject: Re: Revised manuscript - response to your comments

Dear [Reviewer],

Thank you for reading the paper and for setting out the shortcomings so
directly. Two of the three have reshaped the manuscript substantially, and the
third is now stated as a scope limit rather than left implicit. Below is what
changed, and where I have stopped short of what you asked for.


1. Koopman AE and LaSDI are cited but never compared against

You are right, and I have not fixed this by adding the comparison. I would
rather give you the specific reason than an implicit promise.

Both methods fit a latent evolution operator to full-state snapshots of a
system that is observed consistently: LaSDI to solution snapshots of a
parameterized simulation, Koopman autoencoders to repeated observations of a
single autonomous system. C-MAPSS supplies neither. Each unit is a separate,
right-censored trajectory with unknown initial wear, sampled under discrete
operating regimes, observed only through partial noisy sensors, with no shared
parameterization across units and no governing equations. Instantiating either
method here would mean redefining its training setup rather than transferring
it, and a comparison built that way would not be informative about either
approach. This is now stated once, in Section 2.4, as a scope boundary.

What I can offer against the underlying concern - that the gains might reflect
nothing more than avoiding sensor-space recursion - is the milling result
described under point 3. There the sensor-space recursion and the bounded
latent rollout are compared under an identical protocol, and they separate by
several orders of magnitude.


2. Hyperparameter sensitivity and the reduced epoch budget

Addressed in full, in a new appendix. All four parameters you named -
lambda_mono, lambda_smooth, the latent step size, and the regime count - are
swept one at a time around the production defaults, at the full production
budget of 4000 autoencoder epochs, averaged over three seeds, on a
single-regime (FD001) and a multi-regime (FD002) subset. The reduced-budget
concern therefore no longer applies to the sensitivity results, and the metrics
section now states which results use which budget instead of asserting that the
orderings agree.

Three findings are worth flagging, including two that are unflattering:

- lambda_mono is the consequential penalty. On FD002 forecast skill moves from
  -0.09 at lambda_mono = 0 to +0.65 at the production value of 5, while
  reconstruction stays flat until 10, where it begins to erode.

- lambda_smooth is a low-sensitivity knob: across the full range on both
  subsets every metric is unchanged. It is a mild regularizer, not a delicate
  tuning parameter, and the paper now says so rather than implying it was
  tuned.

- The step size separates a stable band from an unstable one rather than
  identifying an optimum, and I have stopped claiming more. Within the stable
  band the production default has the lowest mean and the largest spread of the
  three stable settings on FD001, so the text claims only that it sits inside
  the band.

The regime-count sweep is the one I would draw your attention to. On FD002,
using one regime gives the highest reconstruction in the entire study
(R^2 = 0.986) but negative forecast skill and RUL error above 44 cycles; only
at six regimes, matching the six physical operating conditions, does skill turn
positive and RUL fall to 27 cycles. Reporting it properly also meant reporting
a failure: at three regimes the decoded free-run growth reaches 12.7 and falls
outside our boundedness criterion in all three seeds. An earlier draft omitted
that column. It is now included, with an explanation of why it does not
contradict the boundedness theorem but does fail the stricter empirical
criterion used throughout.


3. Evaluation limited to a single benchmark family

The manuscript now evaluates two run-to-failure benchmarks from different
domains, and the second one is in the main results rather than an appendix.

The PHM 2010 high-speed CNC milling benchmark replaces gas-path measurements
with force, vibration and acoustic-emission channels, and thermodynamic
degradation with progressive tool wear: six cutters run to 315 cuts each,
thirty-five time-domain features per cut, four cutters for training and two
held out. It carries both mechanisms the paper claims.

For stability, the fitted sensor-space companion matrix has spectral radius
1.030, and under a long free run the sensor recursion grows by a factor of
about 9,100 and leaves the boundedness criterion, while every bounded latent
rollout stays controlled and two of them contract. For forecastability, the
paper adds a result that came directly out of this dataset: a decoded latent
forecast carries a fixed reconstruction offset at its forecast origin, which
dominates whenever the per-step change is small. Cancelling it - anchoring the
decoded increment to the last observation - moves horizon-one skill from -263
to -0.73 without changing the latent trajectory at all. This is now Theorem 4
and its corollary, with a proposition giving the condition on the
reconstruction residual under which anchoring is guaranteed to help.

Two things about this result I want to put in front of you rather than leave in
the code. First, the free-run growth depends strongly on how far the rollout is
probed, because a spectral radius of 1.030 needs several hundred steps to
express itself; the paper therefore reports growth at both the data horizon and
the longer horizon used for the C-MAPSS baselines, and states plainly that the
longer probe extends past end of life. Second, cross-domain evidence still
rests on one external benchmark, and the Limitations section says so.

On the bearing dataset specifically, I owe you an explanation rather than a
result. I ran it and left it out. Bearings stay healthy for a long period and
then fail abruptly, so the run-to-failure trajectory is not monotone, and the
monotonicity regularizer on the primary health coordinate is the wrong
inductive bias for it: the health coordinate has to jump rather than progress,
reconstruction was poor, and downstream RUL came out worse than a mean
baseline. That is a real limitation of the method's assumptions rather than a
tuning failure, and it is why the bearing test was dropped rather than
presented. Milling replaced it as the external degradation test because tool
wear is genuinely monotone and therefore within the scope of an algorithm whose
central regularizer assumes monotone degradation. If you would prefer the
bearing result included as a negative case delimiting where that assumption
applies, I am willing to add it, and I take the point that its absence is more
conspicuous than its presence would have been.


Summary of changes

- New main-results section on PHM 2010 CNC milling, reporting free-run growth
  at two probe horizons and forecast skill for every rollout rule.
- New theorem and corollary for anchored decoding, plus a proposition giving
  the residual condition under which it is an improvement.
- New proposition generalising the constant-velocity error bound to the damped
  and windowed extrapolation rules the implementation actually uses; the
  previous bound covered only a rule none of the code used.
- New appendix: full-budget hyperparameter sensitivity, four sweeps, three
  seeds, including the boundedness column and the regime-count failure case.
- Section 2.4 states why Koopman autoencoders and LaSDI are not instantiable
  as baselines in this data setting.
- Abstract, contributions, experimental setup and scope updated to describe a
  two-benchmark study.

Two of your three points are addressed; the first is narrowed but not closed,
and I have tried to be precise about which is which rather than claim a
comparison that does not exist.

Thank you again - the paper is more careful than it was, particularly around
the regime count, the probe horizon, and the limits of the cross-domain
evidence.
