Subject: Re: Revised manuscript — response to your comments

Dear [Reviewer],

Thank you for reading the paper and for setting out the shortcomings so
directly. The three points were fair, and two of them have shaped the revision
substantially. Below is what changed, and where I have stopped short of what
you asked for.


1. Koopman AE and LaSDI are cited but never compared against

You are right, and I have not fixed this in the way you would want. There is
still no head-to-head comparison against Koopman autoencoders or LaSDI in the
revised manuscript, and I would rather say so plainly than dress up a partial
answer as a complete one.

What I have added is the control that isolates the specific concern you raised
— that the gains may just reflect avoiding sensor-space recursion. The new
Appendix G.1 holds the architecture, the context inputs, the training
objective, and the seeds fixed, and varies only the space the dynamics act in:
the same residual MLP learns dynamics either on the frozen bounded latent state
or directly on the standardized sensor vector. The sensor-space head is the
larger of the two (8739 against 4482 parameters on milling, 6027 against 4866
on air quality), so it is not handicapped, and it avoids closed-loop
sensor-space recursion exactly as the latent head does.

The result is conditional, and I report it that way. On PHM milling the bounded
latent head is the only model with positive skill at the longest horizon
(+0.51 at 48 cuts), while the matched sensor-space head is negative at every
horizon past the first — so on that dataset the deflationary explanation
fails: avoiding sensor-space recursion is not by itself enough. On air quality
the deflationary explanation largely holds: the sensor-space head ties the
latent head at 24 hours and beats it at 48, and the sensor VAR is well behaved
to begin with. The honest summary is that the bounded latent space earns its
keep when sensor-space dynamics are difficult and merely provides a guarantee
when they are not.

That control separates the space from the recursion. It does not compare
against methods that learn a latent evolution operator, which is a different
question and the one you actually asked. I have said this explicitly in three
places rather than leaving it implicit — in Related Work, at the end of
Appendix G.1, and in Limitations, where it is now named as the single most
useful experiment the study does not report. If you would prefer that I run it
before endorsement rather than name it as future work, I will.


2. Hyperparameter sensitivity and the reduced epoch budget

This is addressed in full, in the new Appendix F. All four parameters you named
— lambda_mono, lambda_smooth, gamma, and the regime count — are swept
one-at-a-time around the production defaults, at the full production budget of
4000 autoencoder epochs, averaged over three seeds, on a single-regime (FD001)
and a multi-regime (FD002) subset. The reduced-budget concern therefore no
longer applies to the sensitivity results, and Section 5.3 now says which
results use which budget instead of asserting that the orderings agree.

Three findings are worth flagging, including one that is unflattering:

- lambda_mono is the consequential penalty. On FD002, forecast skill moves from
  -0.09 at lambda_mono = 0 to +0.65 at the production value of 5, while
  reconstruction is essentially flat until 10, where it begins to erode.

- lambda_smooth is a low-sensitivity knob. Across the full range on both
  subsets, every metric is unchanged. It is a mild regularizer, not a delicate
  tuning parameter, and I now say so rather than implying it was tuned.

- gamma separates a stable band from an unstable one rather than identifying an
  optimum, and I have stopped claiming more than that. Within the stable band
  the production default 0.05 actually has the lowest mean and the largest
  spread of the three stable settings on FD001 (0.24 +/- 0.38, against
  0.41 +/- 0.09 at 0.02 and 0.43 +/- 0.12 at 0.10). Three seeds do not resolve
  those differences, so the text now claims only that the default sits inside
  the stable band, not that it is the best value in it.

The regime-count sweep is the one I would draw your attention to, because it
turned into the clearest evidence for the paper's own central claim, and
because reporting it properly meant reporting a failure. On FD002, using one
regime gives the *highest* reconstruction in the entire study (R^2 = 0.986) but
negative forecast skill and RUL error above 44 cycles; only at six regimes,
matching the six physical operating conditions, does skill become positive and
RUL fall to 27 cycles. The failure is that at three regimes the decoded
free-run growth reaches 12.7 and falls outside our boundedness criterion in all
three seeds. An earlier draft of the table omitted the boundedness column. It
is now included, along with a paragraph explaining that this does not violate
Theorem 2 — the decoded image of the compact latent box is bounded regardless
— but does fail the stricter empirical growth criterion used throughout the
paper, because a mismatched regime count drives the health coordinate against
the faces of the box. I would rather you see that column than not.


3. Evaluation limited to a single benchmark family

Addressed, though not with the dataset I originally promised. The new Appendix
G evaluates the identical pipeline on two external datasets from different
domains: Beijing multi-site air quality (12 stations, 11 variables, hourly,
cyclic and non-degrading) and PHM 2010 CNC milling (six tools, 35 force,
vibration and acoustic-emission features, steady tool wear).

On the bearing dataset specifically, I owe you an explanation rather than a
result. I ran it, and I have left it out. Bearings stay healthy for a long
period and then fail abruptly, so the run-to-failure trajectory is not
monotone, and the monotonicity regularizer on the primary health coordinate is
the wrong inductive bias for it: the health coordinate has to jump rather than
progress, reconstruction was poor, and downstream RUL came out worse than a
mean baseline. That is a real limitation of the method's assumptions, not a
tuning failure, and it is why the bearing test was dropped rather than
presented. Milling replaced it as the degradation-process external test because
tool wear is genuinely monotone and therefore in scope for an algorithm whose
central regularizer assumes monotone degradation. If you would prefer the
bearing result included as a negative case delimiting where the monotonicity
assumption applies, I am willing to add it, and I take your point that its
absence is more conspicuous than its presence would have been.

I should also flag one way the external evaluation came out weaker than I
expected. Neither external dataset reproduces the *stability separation* seen
on C-MAPSS: the sensor-space VAR stays within the boundedness threshold on both
of them, so these datasets demonstrate stability but not the divergence
contrast. The revised text says this outright instead of implying a contrast
the tables do not support. What does transfer is the forecastability
advantage — positive long-horizon skill against persistence, and on milling the
only positive skill at the 25- and 50-cut horizons. Both datasets also confirm
the short-horizon cost: at horizon one the latent compression discards detail
and simple persistence wins, so the method is aimed at long horizons and the
paper now says so in Limitations.


Summary of changes

- New Appendix F: full-budget hyperparameter sensitivity, four sweeps, three
  seeds, including the boundedness column and the regime-count failure case.
- New Appendix G: external-domain evaluation on air quality and CNC milling.
- New Appendix G.1: matched-capacity latent-versus-sensor-space control.
- Related Work: the relationship to Koopman AE and LaSDI stated precisely, with
  the absent comparison acknowledged.
- Section 5.3: which results use which training budget.
- Section 6.7 and Limitations: the claims that the work was C-MAPSS-only and
  that cross-domain evaluation was future work are removed; the new limitations
  are the missing latent-operator baselines, the absence of RUL labels on the
  external datasets, and the short-horizon cost.
- Section 3.5: the learned bounded residual dynamics head is now defined in the
  method, since the appendices sweep its step size.

Two of your three points are addressed; the first is narrowed but not closed,
and I have tried to be precise about which is which rather than claim the
comparison exists. I am happy to run the Koopman or LaSDI comparison if you
consider it a condition of endorsement.

Thank you again — the paper is more honest than it was, particularly around the
regime count and the limits of the external evidence.
