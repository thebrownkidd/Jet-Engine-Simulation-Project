Dear Editor-in-Chief,

Please find enclosed our manuscript, "Learning Bounded Latent Degradation Dynamics for Stable Rollout and Remaining Useful Life Prediction," submitted for consideration in Engineering Applications of Artificial Intelligence.

This paper addresses a practical stability problem in AI-based prognostics: multivariate degradation forecasts often become unreliable when a model is recursively rolled out beyond its training horizon, which limits their usefulness for real-world remaining-useful-life (RUL) estimation. We propose an algorithm that learns a bounded, low-dimensional latent representation of degradation — combining operating-regime normalization, sensor denoising and selection, and a monotonicity/smoothness-regularized autoencoder — and show theoretically and empirically that this bounded geometry acts as a dynamical regularizer for stable long-horizon rollout.

The AI contribution is a theoretical framework separating the mechanisms that govern rollout behaviour — sensor-space closed-loop divergence, bounded latent rollout, and smoothness-controlled forecastability — together with the algorithm that operationalizes it. Two results extend that framework. First, we give an error bound covering the damped and windowed extrapolation rules that are used in practice, rather than only the textbook constant-velocity rule. Second, we introduce anchored decoding, which cancels the reconstruction offset that a decoded latent forecast carries at its forecast origin, and we state the condition on the reconstruction residual under which anchoring is guaranteed to be an improvement.

The engineering application is validated on two run-to-failure benchmarks from different domains. On the NASA C-MAPSS turbofan benchmark, across four subsets spanning multiple operating regimes and fault modes, the method remains stable under free-run rollout while standard VAR and several neural sequence baselines diverge, and it yields useful downstream RUL predictions (RMSE 14.53-27.58 cycles across subsets). On the PHM 2010 high-speed CNC milling benchmark, which replaces gas-path measurements with force, vibration and acoustic-emission channels and thermodynamic degradation with progressive tool wear, both mechanisms transfer: the fitted sensor-space recursion leaves the boundedness criterion under a long free run while every bounded latent rollout stays controlled, and anchored decoding removes the short-horizon penalty that plain decoding incurs, changing horizon-one forecast skill from -263 to -0.73. We also report a full-budget hyperparameter sensitivity study over the four core parameters of the algorithm.

We believe this work is a good fit for the journal given its focus on validated, practically-motivated AI applications in engineering systems, and its emphasis on reproducible experimental protocols.

This manuscript is original, has not been published previously, and is not under consideration elsewhere. All authors have approved the submission. There are no conflicts of interest to declare, and no external funding was received for this work.
